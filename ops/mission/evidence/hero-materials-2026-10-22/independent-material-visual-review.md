## Car Pose Findings

### 0.0 s: Front Three-Quarter

- A has conspicuous dark/light peppering across the red front fender, upper
  door/shoulder and sloping painted windshield surround. B makes these surfaces
  read more continuously, with less granular breakup around reflected bands.
- B still reads as glossy metallic car paint, not a matte or uniformly smooth
  red replacement. Deep burgundy shadows, vivid red highlight edges, panel
  creases and the sculpted front wheel arch remain legible.
- The broad pale reflection around the front fender/headlamp and the bright
  lower-front patches remain conspicuous. Their persistence prevents treating
  the smoother grain as a lighting or highlight-range solution.
- The darker door region is smoother in B, but does not visibly erase the
  panel's boundary or change the car's silhouette. Wheels, interior, dark roof
  and overall framing show no obvious paired regression.

### 2.0 s: Near-Frontal

- B's hood and painted shoulders show less coarse stippling, particularly in
  red midtones and along the edges of the broad reflected-light bands. This
  second pose supports a real surface-response improvement, not just a favorable
  appearance at the initial angle.
- Fine speckling remains visible in strong highlights. The large near-white
  reflection on the viewer-left front/nose and strong light bands across the
  hood are still dominant; B does not remove them.
- Headlamp shape, center hood contours, lower intake/splitter, windshield and
  red seats remain recognizable and aligned between the pair. B retains the
  dark-to-bright contrast that gives the body its form; no obvious washed-out
  red identity or flattened geometry is visible.

## Card Comparison

- **0.0 seconds:** B's silver face has somewhat clearer tonal variation and
  fine surface streaking than A. This is a restrained improvement, not a dramatic
  transformation. The sweeping groove lines, eight-contact chip pattern, rounded
  corners and thin bright edge remain visible; the face has not become a blank
  white highlight. The coin and emerald rear card show no obvious collateral
  change in identity, placement or silhouette.
- **2.0 seconds:** A's face reads substantially flatter and more uniformly gray.
  B's upper silver region is brighter, with visible brushed streaks and a broad
  gradient into a darker lower region. This variation gives a more convincing
  metal-surface reading rather than merely lifting the whole object uniformly.
  The dark chip boundaries and long engraved curves remain distinguishable.
  I prefer B here; no visible loss of the silver/emerald/coin identity outweighs
  that improvement.
- **Seven-angle sheets, 0/2/3/4/5/6/8.5 seconds:** no gross new silhouette,
  framing, overlap or color-identity regression is apparent in B. At 3 and 6
  seconds the thin card profiles remain recognizable. At 4 seconds the exposed
  silver back is brighter in B, confirming that the response change is not
  confined to the initial front view. The emerald back remains very dark at 5
  seconds in both versions; B does not solve that existing limitation. The
  8.5-second thumbnail is visually consistent with the initial resting pose.

## Card Acceptance Limits

- Accept the anisotropy reduction as a useful controlled refinement, especially
  for the previously flat upper silver face at 2.0 seconds. No rejection-level
  regression is visible in the inspected material.
- The cards still have broad planar areas, fine/faint groove detail and a
  simplified studio-render appearance. More visible brushing is not proof of
  measured metal behavior, physical accuracy or finished photorealism.
- Full-resolution detail was inspected only at 0 and 2 seconds. The other five
  times were screened as contact-sheet thumbnails, not raw full-size frames.
  The sheets cannot establish fine-detail preservation, temporal shimmer or
